## Your voice, as we hear it

Before we write a single headline, we want to make sure we understand how Photon sounds. Here's what we picked up from your deck, your project descriptions, and your existing site:

**Photon sounds like a peer, not a vendor.** You say "SGK brought us in" and "Oura brought us in" — that's a collaborator speaking, not someone selling a service. You lead with what made the shoot interesting, not with the client's name. The work is the story.

**You're direct.** No preamble, no buildup. "The trickiest part was light placement, as there were no windows in the kitchen." That's Photon — specific, honest, straight to the point.

**You're technical without being exclusive.** You reference Astera lighting systems, split diopters, Simera-C, 1000fps — and you never stop to explain them. That works. Your audience knows this language. Explaining it would actually undercut your credibility.

**You're playful when it counts.** "Creating like children and editing like scientists." "Pitch Me Harder." "F it, I'm in!" The space theme — Mission Control, Orbital Mechanic, Chief Photometrics — that's personality baked into the brand, not a gimmick. It works because you commit to it.

**What Photon doesn't sound like:** corporate, inflated, or salesy. No "cutting-edge solutions." No "world-class innovation." No "we're passionate about storytelling." You let the work do that.

### The voice, summarized

| Trait                       | Where Photon lands                                                                                            |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Formal ↔ Casual**         | Casual-leaning. First-person plural. Conversational without being sloppy.                                     |
| **Serious ↔ Playful**       | Mostly serious about the craft, with dry humor underneath. The space theme is the biggest playful commitment. |
| **Reserved ↔ Enthusiastic** | Selectively enthusiastic. "We loved the energy" — never performative, always tied to something specific.      |
| **Simple ↔ Sophisticated**  | Technically sophisticated. Assumes the reader knows what they're looking at.                                  |
| **Warm ↔ Distant**          | Warm enough. "We" and "us" throughout. Collaborative, not transactional.                                      |

**Does this feel right?** If we're missing something — if Photon's voice in your head sounds different from what we've described — now is the time to tell us.

## How the copy needs to work

The site has two jobs: impress the right people, and be findable by them.

Your primary audience — VPs of Brand Marketing, Creative Directors at companies like Nike, Oura, BMW — already knows what a production studio is. They're evaluating whether _Photon_ is the right one. The copy doesn't need to educate. It needs to differentiate.

Your secondary audience — agency producers like your SGK contact — is evaluating infrastructure and reliability. They need to see specs, capacity, and professionalism.

Both audiences are sophisticated readers. They will notice if the copy is generic. They will also notice if it's trying too hard.

### What that means for tone, page by page

The site isn't one tone all the way through. It shifts depending on what the visitor needs in that moment:

| Page type               | Tone                                                      | Why                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Homepage, Work grid** | Minimal. Let the visuals lead. Copy stays out of the way. | Your audience evaluates portfolio quality in seconds. Words can only slow that down.                            |
| **Project pages**       | Specific and technical. Short.                            | "SGK brought us in to shoot..." — that's the model. What was the brief, what was interesting, what did you do.  |
| **About, Philosophy**   | Warm, direct, personal.                                   | This is where the visitor decides if they want to work _with you_. Personality matters.                         |
| **Capabilities**        | Confident, structured, scannable.                         | This is the deck as a web experience. Modular concept explained plainly. Technical modules inventoried clearly. |
| **Rentals**             | Practical, inviting, specs-forward.                       | An agency producer is checking if the space fits their shoot. Give them what they need fast.                    |
| **Contact**             | Simple. No friction.                                      | Name, email, message. That's it.                                                                                |

## Where we're suggesting changes — and why

### 1. CTAs — keeping the personality, adjusting for the audience

Your current CTAs — "Pitch Me Harder," "F it, I'm in!" — are bold and memorable. They work in certain contexts: social, BTS content, creative community touchpoints.

For the main site, we're recommending CTAs that carry the same energy but feel right for a VP of Brand Marketing at Nike deciding whether to reach out:

| Current vibe      | Proposed direction             | Why the shift                                                                             |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Pitch Me Harder" | "Mission briefing starts here" | Keeps the space theme. Feels inviting without being casual about a business relationship. |
| "F it, I'm in!"   | "Let's make something"         | Confident and collaborative. Doesn't assume familiarity with someone you haven't met yet. |
| Generic "Contact" | "Start a conversation"         | Warmer than "Contact." Lower commitment than "Get a Quote."                               |

**We're not losing the personality** — we're adjusting it for the moment. The Archives page, the BTS section, the 404 page? That's where "Pitch Me Harder" energy belongs. The contact form? That's where a creative director needs to feel like a professional conversation is starting.

### 2. Project descriptions — adding structure without losing voice

Your current descriptions are strong but inconsistent in length and depth. Some projects get one sentence. Others get a paragraph. The new site introduces two templates:

**Standard project page:** 2-4 sentences of context (the brief, the approach, what was interesting), plus a BTS section with optional technical callouts.

**Case study page:** Same voice, more depth — the brief, the challenge, the approach, the outcome, plus a client testimonial. These are for your hero relationships: Sorel, Nike, Hyperice, Oura.

The voice stays the same. The structure just gives every project a consistent shape so visitors know what to expect.

### 3. Capabilities page — from deck to narrative

Your deck is dense with great content. On the web, we need to reorganize it so a visitor can scan it in 30 seconds _or_ go deep on a specific capability.

**What's changing:**

The deck organizes by internal department (Motion, Lighting, Effects, Post). That makes sense internally. For the site, we're reorganizing by what the client is looking for:

| Deck structure                                          | Site structure                                   | Why                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Technical departments (Motion, Lighting, Effects, Post) | Production phases (Creative → Production → Post) | A Creative Director thinks in phases: "Can you concept? Can you shoot? Can you finish?" They don't think in departments. |

The actual capabilities stay the same — we're just reframing them around how your client evaluates a production partner.

**What's staying:**

The modular concept is the lead. "A giant network distilled into specialty teams with the mission to maximize resources and elevate what's possible" — that's the opening. The detailed technical modules (8K/6K/4K, slow motion, motion control, anamorphics, pyrotechnics) are still there as scannable inventory. A Creative Director who needs 1000fps wants to see that number, not a paragraph about your "high-speed capabilities."

### 4. Homepage philosophy — one new line

Right now the homepage is all portfolio and logos. We're recommending a short Philosophy section (2-4 sentences, max) that does one thing: tell the visitor _why Photon is different from other studios they're evaluating._

This is also where we suggest adding a single line that acknowledges agency relationships: something like "We work direct with brands and alongside agencies when the project calls for it." This gives agency producers — a real channel for you — a reason to keep scrolling instead of wondering if the site is only for brands.

### 5. Space theme — committed, not forced

The space theme is a real asset. It makes you memorable in a category full of minimal-everything studios. But it works best when it feels natural rather than labored.

**Where the theme should be front and center:**

- Team titles (Mission Control, Chief Photometrics, etc.)
- CTAs ("Mission briefing starts here," "Signal acquired")
- 404 page ("Signal Lost")
- Easter eggs and micro-interactions

**Where it should stay quiet:**

- Project descriptions — these should sound like you talking about the work, not performing a theme
- Capabilities content — specs and services in plain language
- Rentals — agency producers booking studio space want clarity, not metaphor

## SEO and AEO: copy that gets found

Your current site is invisible to search. The rebuild changes that. Here's how copy decisions directly affect discoverability — and why some of our recommendations exist.

### What we're targeting

| Search term                            | Where it lives on the site      | What we need in the copy                                                                                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| "commercial video production Portland" | Homepage, Capabilities          | These words need to appear naturally — in page titles, meta descriptions, and body copy. Not stuffed. Just present.                                          |
| "Portland studio rental"               | Rentals hub, Studio rental page | Dedicated page with the phrase in the heading, specs, and descriptions. This is a real search people make.                                                   |
| "cyclorama studio Portland"            | Studio rental page              | Your 350 sqm cyclorama is a search magnet. The word "cyclorama" should appear in headings and body copy — most people searching this term are ready to book. |
| "high speed cinematography Portland"   | Capabilities page               | A dedicated section on slow-motion / high-speed capability, with specific specs (1000fps+). Specialty terms have low competition — you can own these.        |
| "podcast studio rental Portland"       | Podcast rental page             | Separate page means separate SEO. This is a niche search with low competition and real intent.                                                               |
| "motion control video production"      | Capabilities page               | Same idea. Specific capability + specific page = search visibility.                                                                                          |

### How this shapes the copy

**Page titles and headings matter.** "Our Capabilities" as a page title is invisible to search. "Production Capabilities — Video, Photography, Motion Control" gives Google something to work with while still reading naturally to humans.

**Each page needs to earn its own search result.** This is why Studio Rental and Podcast Rental are separate pages, not tabs on one page. Each one targets a different search query with different intent.

**Project pages are SEO content too.** A project titled "Nike DSG Campaign — Commercial Video Production" performs better in search than "DSG HO23." We can keep the creative title on the page itself while using descriptive titles in metadata.

**AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) is the next layer.** When someone asks an AI assistant "What's the best production studio in Portland for high-speed cinematography?", the answer pulls from structured data on your site. We're building that structure into the code (schema markup, structured headings, FAQ patterns on Capabilities and Rentals) so your content feeds AI answers — not just Google results.

### What this means for how you write project descriptions

Nothing changes about the voice. But we'll add a few fields in the CMS that help with discoverability:

| Field                | Example                                                                                                             | Purpose                                                                              |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **SEO title**        | "Nike DSG Campaign — Commercial Video Production by Photon"                                                         | What Google shows. Different from the creative title.                                |
| **Meta description** | "SGK brought Photon in for Nike's HO23 DSG Campaign. Shot in Portland with high-speed capture and full set design." | 150 characters that show up in search results. Written in your voice.                |
| **Category tags**    | Campaign, Stills, Product, Sport                                                                                    | Not visible to users at launch — but they feed structured data and future filtering. |

You write the project descriptions exactly like you always have. We handle the SEO layer behind the scenes.

## The competitor angle

Here's what we noticed about how production studios in your space handle copy — and where Photon can stand out.

### What most studios do

- **Generic portfolio voice:** "We had the pleasure of working with [Brand] on their latest campaign." This is everywhere. It's polite, forgettable, and interchangeable.
- **Service pages as bullet lists:** A flat list of services with no explanation of _how_ or _why_. No differentiation.
- **No technical specificity:** "We offer high-end video production." High-end how? What camera? What frame rate? What capability do you have that the next studio doesn't?
- **Missing social proof:** Case studies without client quotes. Projects without context. Beautiful work with zero narrative.

### Where Photon's copy stands out

| Competitor pattern                       | Photon's advantage                                               | How we use it                                                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "We had the pleasure of working with..." | "SGK brought us in to shoot..."                                  | Your project voice is already specific and active. We keep it. Every competitor who writes in passive voice makes you look sharper by contrast.                   |
| Flat service lists                       | Modular production system with technical depth                   | The Capabilities page isn't a list — it's a story about how you work, with the technical inventory to back it up.                                                 |
| No specs                                 | 1000fps, motion control, cyclorama, 400 AMP, Astera, anamorphics | You name the tools. That's a trust signal for production-literate buyers. It also feeds SEO for specialty searches.                                               |
| No client voice                          | Case study pages with real testimonials                          | Even one real quote from Sorel or Nike changes the entire credibility equation. This is the biggest gap in the category — and the biggest opportunity for Photon. |
| "About us" = generic mission statement   | Space-themed team with modular philosophy                        | You have a genuine brand identity. Most studios don't. The About page should lean into it.                                                                        |

## Words and phrases

### Use these — they sound like Photon

- "Brought us in" (not "hired us" or "engaged us")
- "Shot in our studio" (ownership, specificity)
- "The trickiest part was..." (honest, craft-focused)
- "We changed things up" (casual confidence)
- Module, modular, production, studio, content, launch, mission
- Technical terms without explanation (1000fps, MOCO, cyclorama, DMX, anamorphic)
- Space/NASA vocabulary where natural (mission, orbit, launch, ground control)

### Avoid these — they don't sound like Photon

- "Solutions" / "deliverables" / "optimize"
- "Cutting-edge" / "state-of-the-art" / "innovative"
- "We're passionate about..." / "We believe in the power of..."
- "World-class" / "best-in-class" (let the roster say that)
- "Leverage" / "synergy" / "stakeholders"
- "We had the pleasure of working with..."
- Any CTA that sounds like a car dealership

**We're keeping:** Your voice, your directness, your technical fluency, the space theme, your project description style.
